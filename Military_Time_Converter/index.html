How military time works

Military time uses a 24-hour day beginning at 0000 and ending at 2359. The first two digits represent the hour and the last two represent the minutes. Because each hour number occurs only once during the day, there is no need to add AM or PM. For example, 0900 means 9:00 AM, while 2100 means 9:00 PM.

Leading zeros are important in compact military notation. Seven-thirty in the morning is written as 0730 rather than 730 when a four-digit format is expected. The converter automatically pads output to four digits so morning hours remain clear and consistent.

Converting military time to regular time

From 0100 through 1159, the hour usually stays the same and receives an AM label. For example, 0845 becomes 8:45 AM. The special case is midnight: 0000 becomes 12:00 AM. Noon is 1200, which becomes 12:00 PM.

For times from 1300 through 2359, subtract 12 from the hour and use PM. Thus 1300 becomes 1:00 PM, 1545 becomes 3:45 PM, and 2310 becomes 11:10 PM. The minute portion does not change during the conversion.

Converting regular time to military time

For AM times from 1:00 AM through 11:59 AM, keep the hour and add a leading zero when needed. For example, 6:20 AM becomes 0620. The exception is 12:00 AM through 12:59 AM: subtract 12 from the hour, making midnight hour 00, so 12:30 AM becomes 0030.

For PM times from 1:00 PM through 11:59 PM, add 12 to the hour. For example, 4:05 PM becomes 1605 and 9:45 PM becomes 2145. Noon is the exception: 12:00 PM remains 1200 because the 12-hour value already matches the 24-hour noon hour.

Midnight, noon, 0000, and 1200

Midnight and noon cause more conversion mistakes than most other times. Midnight is 12:00 AM in the 12-hour system and 00:00 or 0000 in the 24-hour system. Noon is 12:00 PM and corresponds to 12:00 or 1200. Remembering those two anchor points makes most other conversions straightforward.

You may occasionally see 2400 in schedules or operational documents to indicate the end of a calendar day. It can be useful when describing a boundary, but ordinary 24-hour clock notation runs from 00:00 through 23:59. This converter therefore treats 0000 as midnight and does not accept 2400 as a clock input.

Military time versus civilian 24-hour time

The underlying hours and minutes are the same. The biggest visual difference is formatting. Military contexts commonly write four digits without a colon, such as 1845. Civilian 24-hour clocks often use a colon, such as 18:45. The converter displays both so you can move easily between schedules, travel information, healthcare records, logistics, and everyday AM/PM time.

Compact input is normalized

The military-time parser accepts one to four numeric digits and pads shorter compact entries on the left before interpreting them. For example, 30 becomes 0030 and 930 becomes 0930. Colon input such as 9:30 is also accepted. Output military time is always padded to four digits.

This converter uses minute precision only

Seconds are not accepted or displayed. Inputs such as 17:30:45 are outside the current parser. If you are working with logs, timestamps, aviation records, or scientific data that include seconds, preserve those values separately.

2400 is treated as a day-boundary notation, not a clock input

The calculator accepts normalized clock times from 0000 through 2359. Some schedules use 2400 to label the end of a calendar day, but 2400 and 0000 can refer to opposite sides of a date boundary. Because this tool does not track dates, it intentionally avoids silently converting 2400 to 0000.

No date, time-zone, or UTC-offset conversion

The result changes only the notation used to display the same local clock reading. It does not know a date, location, daylight-saving rule, UTC offset, or military time-zone letter. A value such as 1800 remains the same clock reading; only its display changes to 6:00 PM.

For operational schedules, preserve the original date and time-zone context. A four-digit military time by itself does not identify a unique moment worldwide. When ambiguity matters, keep the source date, zone, UTC offset, or other scheduling context with the converted time.

Day-period labels are descriptive only

The Midnight, Morning, Afternoon, Evening, and Night tag is a convenience label generated from the hour. Those labels do not change the conversion result and should not be treated as formal military or scheduling terminology.

Practical Use Cases

Travel and transportation

Airlines, rail systems, transit schedules, and international timetables frequently use the 24-hour clock. A traveler accustomed to AM/PM notation can quickly convert 18:20 to 6:20 PM or verify that 07:05 means 7:05 AM.

Healthcare and medical records

Hospitals and clinical systems often use 24-hour time because it avoids ambiguity between morning and evening. Converting a chart time such as 2145 to 9:45 PM can make records easier to understand for patients and families while preserving the original time reference.

Military, emergency, and public-safety schedules

Military units, emergency services, dispatch systems, and other operations may use compact 24-hour notation for clarity. The converter can help new personnel, students, family members, or anyone reading such schedules become familiar with the notation.

Work schedules and global teams

Some employers use 24-hour schedules to reduce AM/PM mistakes, especially for overnight shifts. Converting 2230 to 10:30 PM or 0545 to 5:45 AM can make shift start and end times easier to interpret.

Education and practice

The reference table and quick examples make the page useful for learning the pattern. Once you know that afternoon hours generally subtract 12 when moving to regular time—and add 12 when moving to military time—the system becomes easy to use mentally.

Important: this is a clock-format converter, not a time-zone converter. Converting 1800 to 6:00 PM does not change the location, date, UTC offset, daylight-saving status, or actual moment in time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is 1300 military time?

1300 is 1:00 PM. For afternoon military times above 1200, subtract 12 from the hour.

What is 0000 in regular time?

0000 is 12:00 AM, or midnight.

What is 1200 in regular time?

1200 is 12:00 PM, or noon.

What is 8:30 PM in military time?

8:30 PM converts to 2030 military time, or 20:30 on a colon-separated 24-hour clock.

Do I need to enter the colon?

No. Military → Regular mode accepts both four-digit forms such as 1730 and colon forms such as 17:30.

Why does military time use leading zeros?

Leading zeros keep compact times at four digits and make morning hours less ambiguous. For example, 7:05 AM is written 0705.

Is 2400 valid?

It is sometimes used to mark the end of a day in schedules, but standard clock notation uses 0000 for midnight. This converter accepts times from 0000 through 2359.

Does this convert time zones too?

No. It changes only the display format between 24-hour and 12-hour notation.

Can I enter 930 instead of 0930?

Yes. Compact numeric input is padded on the left, so 930 is interpreted as 0930 and converts to 9:30 AM.

Can I enter seconds?

No. This converter works at minute precision and accepts hour/minute values only.

Why does the converter reject 2400?

2400 can be used as an end-of-day scheduling notation, but this converter does not track dates. It accepts 0000 through 2359 as clock values so it does not silently erase a possible date-boundary distinction.

Does military time by itself identify a unique moment?

No. A four-digit time such as 1800 does not include a date, time zone, UTC offset, or military zone letter. Keep that context when it matters.

What does “minutes after midnight” mean?

It is the number of whole minutes since 00:00 on the same clock day. For example, 17:30 is 1,050 minutes after midnight.
