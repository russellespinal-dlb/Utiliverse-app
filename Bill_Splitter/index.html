Free Online Bill Splitter for Restaurants, Trips and Group Expenses

The Utiliverse Bill Splitter Calculator helps divide a shared bill without forcing everyone to pay the same amount. Enter the bill subtotal, add a tip percentage, include tax, add each person in the group, and assign custom shares when one person should pay more or less than someone else. The calculator continuously updates the grand total and each person's portion, making it useful for restaurant checks, takeout orders, group trips, shared household purchases, event expenses, and other situations where several people need to divide one total.

Everything is calculated directly in your browser. The current group and bill settings are stored locally on your device so you can return to the page without rebuilding the list each time. No account is required. If you are using a shared or public computer, use the Reset button when you are finished to clear the saved group and restore the default example.

How to Split a Bill with Tip, Tax and Uneven Shares

1. Enter the pre-tip, pre-tax subtotal

Start with the subtotal shown on the receipt before tip and tax are added. For example, if food and drinks total $86.40 before sales tax and gratuity, enter 86.40 as the subtotal. The calculator treats the subtotal as the base amount for both tax and tip. This is a simple and common approach for group splitting. If your receipt already includes gratuity, set the tip percentage to zero and include that existing gratuity in the subtotal instead.

2. Choose a tip percentage or type your own

Use one of the quick-tip buttons for a common percentage or enter any percentage manually. The tip amount updates immediately. A 20% tip on a $100 subtotal adds $20 to the shared total. Because the calculator applies tip to the subtotal rather than the subtotal plus tax, the calculation stays predictable and easy to verify.

3. Add the sales-tax percentage

Enter the tax percentage shown on the receipt or the applicable local rate. The calculator multiplies the subtotal by the tax percentage and adds that amount to the final bill. The bill summary shows subtotal, tip, tax, and grand total separately so you can compare the calculation with the receipt.

4. Add everyone who is sharing the bill

Each participant gets a name, a share value, and an optional amount-paid field. Add as many people as needed. The names are used throughout the results and settlement section, so descriptive labels such as Alex, Maria, Dad, Roommate 1, or Team A can make the final instructions easier to understand.

5. Use one share each for a simple even split

For an equal split, give every person exactly one share. If four people each have one share, there are four total shares and each person receives 25% of the grand total. The “Set All to 1 Share” button resets the group to this simple arrangement.

6. Use custom shares for an uneven split

Shares let you make the division proportional without entering every menu item. A person with two shares pays twice as much as a person with one share. Someone with half a share pays half as much as a one-share participant. Decimal shares also work, so you can fine-tune the balance when one person had a more expensive meal or skipped part of a shared order.

7. Enter what each person actually paid

The amount-paid field is optional, but it unlocks the “Who Owes Whom” calculation. Enter the amount each person covered at the restaurant or checkout. If one person paid the entire bill, enter the full payment for that person and zero for everyone else. If two people split payment between cards, enter the amount charged to each card.

8. Follow the settlement instructions

The settlement section produces a compact set of transfers that brings everyone back to their intended share. A person who paid less than they owe sends money to someone who paid more than they owe. This is useful after one person picks up the restaurant check, when several friends pay different parts of a vacation expense, or when one roommate temporarily covers a shared purchase.

Practical Use Cases for the Bill Splitter

Restaurant dinners with different appetites

Equal splitting is quick, but it can feel unfair when one guest orders significantly more food, drinks, or extras. Custom shares offer a middle ground between perfectly itemizing the receipt and dividing everything evenly. Give a larger share to someone whose portion was notably higher and a smaller share to someone who ordered less. Tip and tax follow the same proportion.

Family meals with children

A child may reasonably count as half a share when adults are covering full meals. Instead of manually subtracting individual items, set each adult to one share and the child to 0.5. Families can adjust that value depending on what was ordered.

Group trips and shared travel expenses

The same logic works beyond restaurants. Use the subtotal for a hotel add-on, rideshare, grocery run, fuel purchase, attraction tickets, or another shared cost. Set tip and tax to zero when they do not apply. Then assign shares according to how the group wants to divide the expense and enter who actually paid.

Roommates sharing household purchases

Roommates often split cleaning supplies, groceries, internet equipment, or small household purchases. If everyone uses an item equally, use one share each. Enter the roommate who paid at checkout to see who should reimburse them.

One person pays with a rewards card

Groups sometimes let one person pay the entire bill so they can earn credit-card points or simplify checkout. Enter the full payment for that person and zero for the others. The calculator will show each participant's intended portion and create transfers back to the payer.

Splitting a bill when several people paid

Sometimes one person covers food, another adds cash toward the tip, and someone else pays part of the total with a second card. Enter each actual contribution in the amount-paid fields. The settlement calculation takes all those payments into account rather than assuming there was a single payer.

Choosing the right level of precision

Shares are designed for practical proportional splitting, not detailed receipt itemization. If exact fairness depends on every appetizer, drink, coupon, surcharge, or individually taxed item, an item-by-item expense tracker may be more appropriate. For many everyday group bills, proportional shares strike a useful balance between speed and fairness.

Bill Splitter FAQ

How do I split a restaurant bill evenly?

Add every person and leave each person at one share. The calculator divides the grand total evenly across the group.

Can I split the bill unevenly?

Yes. Give each person a different number of shares. Two shares costs twice as much as one share, while 0.5 shares costs half as much.

Does the calculator include tip and tax?

Yes. Tip and tax are calculated from the subtotal and then included in the grand total before the total is distributed by shares.

How does the “Who Owes Whom” calculation work?

The calculator compares each person's fair share with the amount they actually paid. People who underpaid reimburse people who covered more than their intended portion.

Can multiple people pay part of the bill?

Yes. Enter the actual payment made by each person. The settlement calculation uses all payment amounts when determining transfers.

Can I use a currency other than dollars?

Yes. Change the currency symbol field to another symbol or short label, such as €, £, ¥, ₹ or CAD.

Does Utiliverse upload my bill information?

No. The calculations run locally in your browser. The saved group is stored in your browser's local storage and is not submitted to a Utiliverse server by this tool.

Why might my receipt differ by a cent?

Restaurants may round tax, gratuity, discounts or individual line items differently. This calculator rounds participant amounts to cents while keeping the calculated participant total aligned with the grand total.
