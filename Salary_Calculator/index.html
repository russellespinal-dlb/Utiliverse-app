How to Use the Salary Calculator

This calculator converts one gross-pay figure into common pay frequencies so different compensation formats can be compared on the same basis. Enter the amount, choose whether it is hourly, daily, weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, monthly, quarterly or annual, and review the equivalents.

Gross pay onlyThe calculator does not estimate federal, state or local taxes, payroll withholding, insurance, retirement deductions or other take-home-pay items.
52-week annualizationHourly and daily inputs are annualized using 52 weeks. Weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, monthly and quarterly figures use 52, 26, 24, 12 and 4 periods respectively.
Adjusted workday modelEntered holidays and vacation days reduce the modeled workdays for comparison purposes. This is not automatically a payroll deduction.
No overtime modelingThe calculator treats the entered hourly rate as a straight-time rate. It does not separately calculate overtime premiums, bonuses, commissions or tips.

Salary Conversion Formulas

The tool first converts the entered amount to an annual gross equivalent. Hourly pay uses hourly rate × hours per week × 52. Daily pay uses daily rate × workdays per week × 52. Weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, monthly and quarterly pay use 52, 26, 24, 12 and 4 periods per year respectively.

Worked example using the current defaults

With the current default inputs — $30/hour, 40 hours per week, 5 workdays per week, 11 holidays and 10 vacation days — the unadjusted annual equivalent is $62,400.00.

The working-day baseline is 260 days. Subtracting 21 entered non-working days leaves 239 modeled workdays. At 8 hours per day, the adjusted comparison value is approximately $57,360.00.

The adjusted figure is a comparison model, not a payroll forecast. If holidays and vacation are paid, a salaried employee's stated annual salary normally does not fall simply because those days are taken. The adjusted column is most useful for comparing paid-time assumptions, contractor availability or effective working-day value.

Gross pay vs. take-home pay

Gross compensation is the amount before taxes and deductions. Take-home pay depends on factors the calculator does not ask for, including tax jurisdiction, filing status, payroll-tax treatment, insurance, retirement contributions, garnishments and other deductions. Because those factors vary widely, this page intentionally stops at gross-pay conversion.

Biweekly vs. semi-monthly pay

Biweekly pay is usually every two weeks, producing 26 pay periods in a 52-week year. Semi-monthly pay is typically twice per month, producing 24 pay periods. Two jobs with the same annual salary can therefore have different individual check amounts depending on the pay schedule.

Hourly pay, overtime and exempt status

This calculator does not determine overtime eligibility or legal classification. In the United States, the Fair Labor Standards Act generally requires overtime pay for covered nonexempt employees at at least one and one-half times the regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek, but exemptions and special rules can apply. Use the calculator's hourly conversion as straight-time annualization unless you separately account for overtime.

Bonuses, commissions, tips and benefits

The calculator converts the compensation amount you enter; it does not add bonuses, commissions, tips, stock awards, employer retirement contributions, health coverage or other benefits. When comparing job offers, total compensation can differ substantially even when base salary is similar.

Employees vs. contractors

Contractors can use the tool to annualize hourly or daily rates, but a contractor's quoted rate is not directly equivalent to an employee salary. Contractors may need to cover business expenses, unpaid administrative time, insurance, equipment, downtime and taxes themselves. Worker classification also depends on the facts of the relationship rather than simply whether someone is paid hourly or by invoice.

Paid vs. unpaid time off

For an hourly worker who is not paid for time away, holidays or vacation can reduce actual annual earnings. For an employee whose salary continues during paid leave, those days typically do not reduce stated salary. The calculator's adjusted column does not know whether a given day is paid, so interpret that number according to the arrangement you are modeling.

Why 52 weeks is an approximation

Annualizing with 52 weeks is a standard planning convention, but a calendar year is slightly longer than exactly 52 weeks. Payroll systems may also use specific pay-period calendars, leap years, partial-year employment, unpaid leave or an occasional 27th biweekly payroll in ways this simplified converter does not model.

Use the output as a comparison baseline. For an actual offer, payroll statement or employment agreement, use the employer's stated compensation terms and the applicable payroll rules rather than treating this calculator as the authoritative payroll record.

Salary Calculator FAQ

What does this salary calculator do?

It converts gross compensation between hourly, daily, weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, monthly, quarterly and annual amounts.

How does hourly pay convert to annual salary?

Hourly pay is multiplied by the entered hours per week and by 52 weeks.

What is biweekly pay?

Biweekly pay generally occurs every two weeks, producing 26 pay periods in a 52-week year.

What is semi-monthly pay?

Semi-monthly pay normally occurs twice per month, producing 24 pay periods per year.

How does the adjusted value work?

The adjusted view uses 52 weeks multiplied by your entered workdays per week as the annual workday baseline, then subtracts the holiday and vacation days you enter.

Does this calculate take-home pay?

No. It estimates gross compensation only and does not calculate taxes, insurance, retirement contributions or other payroll deductions.

Can contractors use this calculator?

Yes. Contractors can annualize hourly or daily rates, but business expenses, self-employment taxes, benefits and unpaid time should be considered separately.

Does paid time off reduce my stated salary?

Normally no. The adjusted column is a working-day comparison tool, not an employer payroll deduction when holidays or vacation are paid.

Does the calculator include overtime pay?

No. Hourly pay is annualized at the entered straight-time rate. Overtime eligibility and overtime premiums depend on applicable law and the worker's classification.

Does the calculator include bonuses, commissions or benefits?

No. The calculator converts the pay amount you enter and does not automatically add bonuses, commissions, tips, stock compensation, employer retirement contributions, health insurance or other benefits.

Why can contractor pay not be compared directly with employee salary?

Contractors may need to cover business expenses, unpaid time, insurance, equipment, taxes and benefits themselves. The calculator can annualize the rate but does not estimate those additional costs.

Why does the calculator use 52 weeks?

It uses 52 weeks as a standard annualization convention. Actual payroll calendars can differ because of partial years, leap years, unpaid leave or an occasional extra biweekly pay period.
