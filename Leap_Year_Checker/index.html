What makes a year a leap year?

The Gregorian calendar does not simply add a leap day every four years. The actual rule has three parts. First, a year divisible by 4 is normally a leap year. Second, a year divisible by 100 is normally not a leap year. Third, a year divisible by 400 is a leap year after all.

That is why 2024 is a leap year, 2100 is not, and 2000 is. The year 2024 is divisible by 4 and is not a century year. The year 2100 is divisible by both 4 and 100, but not by 400. The year 2000 is divisible by 400, which restores leap-year status.

Why leap years are necessary

A calendar year of exactly 365 days is slightly shorter than Earth's actual trip around the Sun. The tropical year is approximately 365.242 days. If every calendar year contained exactly 365 days, the calendar would slowly drift relative to the seasons.

Adding February 29 in selected years keeps the calendar much closer to the astronomical year. The Gregorian century exceptions improve the older every-four-years rule by removing some excess leap days. Over a 400-year Gregorian cycle, there are 97 leap years and 303 common years.

Why century years are different

Using only a divisible-by-4 rule would make the average calendar year 365.25 days long, which is slightly too long. The Gregorian system therefore skips leap day in most century years. Years such as 1700, 1800, 1900, and 2100 are not leap years even though they are divisible by 4.

Every fourth century is kept as a leap year because years divisible by 400 qualify. That makes 1600, 2000, and 2400 leap years and gives the Gregorian calendar an average of 365.2425 days per year.

How many days are in a leap year?

A leap year contains 366 days instead of the usual 365. The additional day is February 29, giving February 29 days instead of 28. All other month lengths remain unchanged.

The extra day can affect date arithmetic, anniversaries, recurring events, payroll calculations, software tests, and any logic that assumes a year always has the same number of days.

Leap years and software development

Leap years are a classic source of date-related software bugs. Code that assumes every year is exactly 365 days can produce incorrect age calculations, expiration dates, day counts, and recurring schedules. Correct implementations must account for February 29 and the century exceptions.

A compact version of the Gregorian rule is: a year is leap if it is divisible by 400, or if it is divisible by 4 but not by 100. That condition is suitable for many programming languages and matches the rule used by modern calendar systems.

Historical calendar caution

The Gregorian calendar was introduced in 1582, but countries adopted it at different times. Earlier dates may have been recorded under the Julian calendar or another local system. Applying Gregorian rules backward to earlier years is called using a proleptic Gregorian calendar.

For ordinary modern calculations, that convention is convenient. For historical research, however, you may need to know which calendar was actually in use in the place and period being studied.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if a year is a leap year?

A Gregorian year is a leap year if it is divisible by 4, except century years must also be divisible by 400.

Is 2024 a leap year?

Yes. 2024 is divisible by 4 and is not divisible by 100, so it has 366 days.

Is 2100 a leap year?

No. 2100 is divisible by 100 but not by 400, so it is a common year.

Is 2000 a leap year?

Yes. 2000 is divisible by 400.

How many days are in a leap year?

366 days. February contains 29 days instead of 28.

Why do we have leap years?

They keep the calendar aligned with Earth's approximately 365.242-day orbit around the Sun.
