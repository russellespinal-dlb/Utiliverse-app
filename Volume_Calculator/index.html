How to Use the Volume Calculator

Select the card for the shape you want to calculate, enter the requested dimensions, and choose Calculate. Keep every input in the same length unit. If you enter feet, the result is cubic feet; if you enter centimeters, the result is cubic centimeters.

Volume Formulas Used

Rectangular prism: V = length × width × height

Cylinder: V = π × radius² × height

Sphere: V = 4/3 × π × radius³

Cone: V = 1/3 × π × radius² × height

Rectangular Prism and Cube Volume

A rectangular prism uses three dimensions: length, width and height. Multiply all three values to find the amount of three-dimensional space inside the shape. A cube is a special rectangular prism where all three dimensions are equal, so the same calculator works for both.

Cylinder Volume

A cylinder uses its radius and height. The calculator first squares the radius, multiplies by π, and then multiplies by the height. The radius is half the diameter, so if you only know the diameter, divide it by two before entering the value.

Sphere Volume

A sphere needs only its radius. The formula uses the radius cubed and multiplies by 4/3 × π. Because the radius is raised to the third power, even a small change in radius can produce a much larger change in volume.

Cone Volume

A cone with the same base radius and height as a cylinder has one-third of that cylinder's volume. Enter the cone's radius and perpendicular height to calculate its cubic volume.

Using Consistent Units

This calculator does not convert units automatically. Use the same unit for every dimension in a calculation. Mixing feet and inches, for example, produces a meaningless result unless you convert them to one common unit first. The output is labeled “cubic units” because the specific unit depends on what you entered.

How to calculate cubic volume

Volume measures three-dimensional space, so the answer is expressed in cubic units. If the dimensions are in inches, the result is cubic inches; if they are in feet, the result is cubic feet; if they are in centimeters, the result is cubic centimeters.

Cube and rectangular prism volume

A cube is a rectangular prism with equal length, width, and height. For a cube with side length 5, the volume is 5 × 5 × 5 = 125 cubic units. For a rectangular box measuring 8 × 4 × 3, the volume is 96 cubic units.

Cylinder volume from radius and height

For a cylinder, use the radius of the circular base—not the diameter. If the diameter is 10, the radius is 5. With radius 5 and height 8, the formula is π × 5² × 8, which is about 628.3185 cubic units.

Sphere volume from radius

A sphere needs only the radius. For radius 3, the volume is 4/3 × π × 3³, which is about 113.0973 cubic units.

Cone volume from radius and height

A cone with the same base radius and perpendicular height as a cylinder has one-third of that cylinder's volume. Do not substitute slant height for perpendicular height.

Cubic feet, cubic inches, cubic meters, and other units

The calculator does not convert between measurement systems. Instead, the unit follows the inputs: feet produce cubic feet (ft³), inches produce cubic inches (in³), meters produce cubic meters (m³), and centimeters produce cubic centimeters (cm³). Convert all dimensions to one consistent unit before entering them.

Calculation Methodology and Shape Assumptions

The calculator applies standard geometric formulas directly in JavaScript using the dimensions you provide. Results are displayed with up to four decimal places and labeled as cubic units because the page does not assume a particular measurement system.

Rectangular prismUses length × width × height. A cube is the special case where all three dimensions are equal.

Radius and diameter are not interchangeable

The cylinder, sphere and cone calculators expect a radius. If your measurement is a diameter, divide it by two before entering it. Entering the full diameter as the radius can greatly overstate the volume because radius is squared or cubed in these formulas.

Use perpendicular height for cylinders and cones

Height means the perpendicular distance from one base plane to the opposite end or apex. A cone's slant height is longer than its perpendicular height and should not be entered into the height field unless it happens to be equal in a special case.

Zero dimensions and negative dimensions

A zero dimension is accepted and produces a volume of zero, which is mathematically consistent for a collapsed shape. Negative dimensions are rejected because the calculator treats dimensions as physical lengths rather than signed coordinates.

Units must stay consistent

If dimensions are entered in centimeters, the result is cubic centimeters; if they are entered in feet, the result is cubic feet. Mixing units such as feet and inches in the same calculation produces a result that has no meaningful single cubic unit until the dimensions are converted to one common unit first.

This calculator models ideal geometric solids. Real containers and objects may have wall thickness, rounded corners, tapered sides, irregular surfaces, internal obstructions, or manufacturing tolerances that reduce usable capacity.

Precision and π

Circular-shape calculations use JavaScript's Math.PI. The displayed result is rounded to at most four decimal places. JavaScript Number arithmetic is suitable for ordinary geometry calculations, but extremely large or unusually precise measurements can eventually encounter floating-point limitations.

Volume Calculator FAQ

How do I calculate volume?

Choose the shape, enter its required dimensions using one consistent length unit, and apply the appropriate formula. The result is expressed in cubic units.

How do I calculate the volume of a cube?

For a cube, multiply the side length by itself three times: V = s³.

How do I calculate the volume of a rectangular prism?

Multiply length by width by height: V = l × w × h.

How do I calculate the volume of a cylinder?

Use V = πr²h, where r is the radius of the circular base and h is the perpendicular height.

How do I calculate the volume of a sphere?

Use V = 4/3 × πr³, where r is the sphere's radius.

How do I calculate the volume of a cone?

Use V = 1/3 × πr²h, where r is the base radius and h is the perpendicular height.

What units should I use?

Use any consistent length unit, such as inches, feet, centimeters, or meters. Use the same unit for every dimension in one calculation.

Why is volume measured in cubic units?

Volume measures three-dimensional space, so a length unit is multiplied across three dimensions. Feet become cubic feet, inches become cubic inches, and meters become cubic meters.

Can this calculate cubic feet?

Yes. Enter every dimension in feet and interpret the result as cubic feet.

Can this calculate cubic inches?

Yes. Enter every dimension in inches and interpret the result as cubic inches.

Can this calculate cubic meters or cubic centimeters?

Yes. Enter all dimensions in meters for cubic meters, or centimeters for cubic centimeters.

Should I enter radius or diameter?

Enter the radius for cylinders, spheres, and cones. If you know the diameter, divide it by two first.

Can I use slant height for a cone?

No. The cone formula requires the perpendicular height from the base plane to the apex.

Can I enter decimal measurements?

Yes. The dimension fields accept decimal values.

Does this calculate liters or gallons?

Not directly. This calculator returns geometric cubic volume. Use a compatible unit converter afterward if you need liters, gallons, or another capacity unit.

Does this calculate the usable capacity of a real container?

It calculates ideal geometric volume. Wall thickness, rounded corners, tapers, internal components, and manufacturing tolerances can make real usable capacity different.
