How to Use the Volume Calculator

Select the card for the shape you want to calculate, enter the requested dimensions, and choose Calculate. Keep every input in the same length unit. If you enter feet, the result is cubic feet; if you enter centimeters, the result is cubic centimeters.

Volume Formulas Used

Rectangular prism: V = length × width × height

Cylinder: V = π × radius² × height

Sphere: V = 4/3 × π × radius³

Cone: V = 1/3 × π × radius² × height

Rectangular Prism and Cube Volume

A rectangular prism uses three dimensions: length, width and height. Multiply all three values to find the amount of three-dimensional space inside the shape. A cube is a special rectangular prism where all three dimensions are equal, so the same calculator works for both.

Cylinder Volume

A cylinder uses its radius and height. The calculator first squares the radius, multiplies by π, and then multiplies by the height. The radius is half the diameter, so if you only know the diameter, divide it by two before entering the value.

Sphere Volume

A sphere needs only its radius. The formula uses the radius cubed and multiplies by 4/3 × π. Because the radius is raised to the third power, even a small change in radius can produce a much larger change in volume.

Cone Volume

A cone with the same base radius and height as a cylinder has one-third of that cylinder's volume. Enter the cone's radius and perpendicular height to calculate its cubic volume.

Using Consistent Units

This calculator does not convert units automatically. Use the same unit for every dimension in a calculation. Mixing feet and inches, for example, produces a meaningless result unless you convert them to one common unit first. The output is labeled “cubic units” because the specific unit depends on what you entered.

Common Uses for Volume Calculations

Volume calculations can help estimate container capacity, room or box space, material quantities, tank dimensions, concrete forms, science problems, packaging, landscaping and other three-dimensional measurements. For liquid capacity or material purchasing, you may still need to convert the cubic result into liters, gallons, cubic yards or another practical unit.

Volume Calculator FAQ

What shapes can this volume calculator calculate?

This calculator supports rectangular prisms and cubes, cylinders, spheres and cones.

What units should I use?

Use any consistent length unit such as inches, feet, centimeters or meters. Use the same unit for every dimension in one calculation. The result is returned in that unit cubed.

What is the formula for a rectangular prism?

Volume equals length multiplied by width multiplied by height: V = l × w × h.

What is the formula for a cylinder?

Cylinder volume is V = πr²h, where r is the radius and h is the height.

What is the formula for a sphere?

Sphere volume is V = 4/3 × πr³, where r is the radius.

What is the formula for a cone?

Cone volume is V = 1/3 × πr²h, where r is the radius and h is the height.

Can I enter decimal measurements?

Yes. The dimension fields accept decimal values, which is useful for measurements such as 2.5 feet or 7.25 centimeters.

Are my measurements saved or uploaded?

No. The volume calculations run locally in your browser and the measurements are not submitted to a Utiliverse server by this tool.
