Uppercase, lowercase, and inverse case

UPPERCASE converts letters that have an uppercase form into that form, while lowercase performs the reverse. Numbers, punctuation, symbols, spaces, and line breaks remain unchanged. Inverse case is different: it examines each cased character and flips uppercase letters to lowercase and lowercase letters to uppercase. Characters that do not have a meaningful upper/lower distinction are left as they are. Alternating case applies a repeating lower-uppercase pattern across detected letters while leaving non-letter characters in place.

Title case versus capitalized case

Capitalized Case is deliberately simple: every detected word begins with an uppercase letter and the remaining cased letters are lowercased. Title Case uses a more editorial approach for English text. Common short function words such as “a,” “an,” “the,” “and,” “or,” “but,” “in,” “on,” “at,” “to,” “for,” “of,” “by,” and “as” remain lowercase when they appear in the middle of a title, while the first and last detected words are capitalized. Different publishers use different title-case style guides, so the result should be treated as a practical default rather than a replacement for a publication's house style.

Sentence case

Sentence case lowercases the input first and then capitalizes the first detected letter at the beginning of the text, after sentence-ending punctuation, and at the beginning of a new line. This is useful when a paragraph arrives in all caps or inconsistent capitalization and needs a readable baseline. Proper nouns, acronyms, trademarks, product names, and specialized abbreviations cannot be inferred reliably from capitalization alone, so sentence-case output may still need human review.

Developer naming conventions

Software projects frequently use naming conventions that remove ordinary spaces and punctuation. camelCase begins with a lowercase word and capitalizes each later word, as in customerOrderTotal. PascalCase capitalizes every component, as in CustomerOrderTotal. snake_case joins lowercase words with underscores, kebab-case uses hyphens, dot.case uses periods, and path/case uses forward slashes. CONSTANT_CASE uses uppercase words joined by underscores, while Header-Case capitalizes the components and joins them with hyphens.

For these identifier-style conversions, the tool first identifies Unicode letter-and-number tokens and then normalizes their separators. This means punctuation that is meaningful in natural-language prose may be removed when creating an identifier. That behavior is intentional: a phrase such as “Customer order: #42” becomes a normalized series of word-like tokens rather than preserving the colon and hash mark. When exact punctuation must remain, use one of the natural-language transformations instead.

Unicode and language considerations

Modern JavaScript case conversion understands far more than ASCII, and this tool uses Unicode-aware token matching for its identifier transformations. Accented Latin letters and many characters from other writing systems are therefore handled more reliably than with an A–Z-only regular expression. Even so, capitalization rules are language-dependent. Turkish dotted and dotless I, German sharp S, Greek sigma forms, and scripts without upper/lower case distinctions are examples of why one universal casing algorithm cannot perfectly match every locale or editorial rule. Review important multilingual text after conversion.

Practical Use Cases

Cleaning headings and copied text

Text copied from PDFs, spreadsheets, emails, older systems, or forms often arrives in ALL CAPS or inconsistent mixed case. Lowercase, sentence case, and title case can quickly create a cleaner starting point without manually editing every word. The character, word, line, and sentence counters also provide a quick view of the amount of text being processed.

Preparing code variables and configuration keys

Developers frequently receive labels in human-readable form and need to turn them into consistent identifiers. A phrase such as “Account notification preference” can become accountNotificationPreference, AccountNotificationPreference, account_notification_preference, or account-notification-preference depending on the conventions of the language, framework, API, database, or configuration format in use. The converter makes those variations easy to compare.

Creating filenames, slugs, and structured labels

Kebab case and snake case are often useful for filenames, URL-like labels, export columns, and structured datasets. The converter normalizes visible word separators, but it is not a complete URL slug generator: it does not guarantee transliteration, reserved-character handling, filesystem compatibility, or uniqueness. If a platform imposes specific filename or URL rules, validate the final result against those rules before publishing.

Editing documentation and marketing copy

Writers can use title case for draft headings, sentence case for body copy that arrived in all caps, or capitalized case for labels where every word is intentionally emphasized. Marketing teams can normalize campaign labels, button text, spreadsheet headings, and content inventories. Because house styles differ, editorial judgment should still be applied to brand names, acronyms, proper nouns, and headline conventions.

Working with bulk lists and multi-line text

The converter preserves line breaks for natural-language transformations, making it useful for lists, notes, and blocks of text. Optional line trimming removes whitespace at the beginning and end of each line, while the repeated-space option reduces runs of spaces and tabs without deleting line breaks. Identifier modes normalize the full text into tokens, so they are better suited to a phrase or label than to a long multi-paragraph document.

Frequently Asked Questions

What text case formats does this converter support?

It supports uppercase, lowercase, title case, sentence case, capitalized case, alternating case, inverse case, camelCase, PascalCase, snake_case, kebab-case, CONSTANT_CASE, dot.case, path/case, and Header-Case.

What is the difference between Title Case and Capitalized Case?

Title Case keeps common short English function words lowercase when they appear in the middle of a heading, while Capitalized Case simply capitalizes the first letter of every detected word.

Does sentence case know which words are proper nouns?

No. Sentence case can identify likely sentence starts, but it cannot reliably infer names, acronyms, brands, or specialized capitalization from plain text alone. Review those items after conversion.

Will punctuation and line breaks be preserved?

They are preserved by natural-language modes such as uppercase, lowercase, title case, sentence case, capitalized case, alternating case, and inverse case. Identifier modes intentionally normalize separators and punctuation.

Does the tool support accented and non-English letters?

It uses Unicode-aware matching and JavaScript's built-in case conversion, so many non-ASCII letters are supported. Locale-specific capitalization rules can still differ, so important multilingual text should be reviewed.

What do “Trim line edges” and “Collapse repeated spaces” do?

Trim line edges removes whitespace from the beginning and end of each line. Collapse repeated spaces reduces consecutive spaces or tabs inside a line to one space. Neither option removes line breaks.

Is kebab-case output automatically safe as a URL slug?

Not necessarily. Kebab case is a naming format, not a complete slug-validation system. A publishing platform may require additional transliteration, reserved-character rules, normalization, length limits, or uniqueness checks.

Can I save the converted result?

Yes. Use Copy Output to place the result on the clipboard or Download TXT to save the current converted output as a UTF-8 plain-text file.
